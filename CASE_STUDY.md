# Case Study: NIMCET 2026 Digital Marketing Live Project

## 1\. Background

NIMCET 2026 is the common entrance examination for MCA admissions across participating NITs and IIITs. The official documentation used in this repository shows a live admission cycle running from early March to late July 2026, with the application window open from **03 March 2026 to 01 May 2026**, the test scheduled for **06 June 2026**, and a total of **1,300 seats** across participating institutes.

This case study documents the marketing-side work and the permitted evidence available during the live project.

> \*\*Public documentation boundary:\*\* Only permitted details are shown in this repository. Other intricate details, including account identifiers, full account exports, exact targeting settings, keyword-level details, audience lists, creative internals, budget-control logic, and applicant-level data, are confidential.

## 2\. What the project needed to solve

The project was fundamentally an **awareness-to-application** problem.

Potential applicants first needed to understand:

* what NIMCET is
* whether they were eligible
* which institutes participate
* what the dates and deadlines were
* why the MCA route through NIMCET is worth considering
* how to act before the application window closed

That means the marketing task was not just promotion. It required **clarity, trust, timing, intent capture, and urgency management**.

## 3\. Audience definition

The target audience documented in the project materials was:

* Indian nationals
* graduates from a minimum three-year UG programme with Mathematics or Statistics as one of the subjects
* B.E. / B.Tech graduates from a recognised university or institution
* candidates meeting the required academic eligibility thresholds, including applicable category relaxations

This made the project a focused higher-education demand-generation case rather than a broad consumer campaign.

## 4\. Campaign architecture

The working notes structured the campaign into **five sequential phases**, each aligned with a different stage of applicant psychology:

|Phase|Objective|Core message role|
|-|-|-|
|Awareness Campaign|Make students aware that registration had started|Inform and introduce|
|Opportunity Campaign|Frame MCA through NIMCET as a worthwhile career route|Build aspiration and relevance|
|Consideration Campaign|Reduce friction and help serious prospects move towards registration|Clarify and reassure|
|Urgency Campaign|Bring deadline pressure into the message|Remind and accelerate action|
|Final Push Campaign|Capture last-minute applicants before closure|Convert high-intent late movers|

This structure avoided treating all marketing messages as identical. The communication need at the start of a form window was different from the communication need near the deadline.

## 5\. Channel approach in the available materials

The project notes indicate a combination of:

* Google Ads
* organic social content
* institution-led content distribution
* possible video-led authority assets, including HOD-led explanatory content
* supporting formats such as posters, infographics, FAQs, eligibility explainers, and deadline creatives

The materials favoured a professional, informational tone rather than trend-led content.

## 6\. Registration outcome

The campaign cycle had a stated registration target of **18,000 registrations**.

|Target|Final outcome|
|-:|-:|
|18,000 registrations|19,000+ registrations|

## 9\. Organic content logic

The content notes show a clear informational structure:

* what NIMCET is
* who is eligible
* which institutes participate
* key dates and deadlines
* application flow
* programme value and career outcomes
* student, alumni, faculty, and authority-led credibility assets

This was appropriate for an exam-led campaign because users often move through:

**awareness → self-check → reassurance → urgency → application**
